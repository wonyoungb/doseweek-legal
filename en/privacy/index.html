DoseWeek · iOS 1.0.5

Privacy Policy

This text takes effect on October 3, 2026. The parts about accounts, sync, Plus, ads and other new features describe the first DoseWeek version that has them (the next iOS update) and apply from the release of that version. Earlier versions do not have these features. DoseWeek is a personal record-keeping tool for injection tracking. This policy explains what information the app handles, where it goes and what you can choose, including ads in the free version and Plus purchases.

1. Where your data is stored

This text takes effect on October 3, 2026. The parts about accounts, sync, Plus, ads and other new features describe the first DoseWeek version that has them (the next iOS update) and apply from the release of that version. Earlier versions do not have these features.

You may optionally sign in to a DoseWeek account with Apple or Google. Existing local-record features remain available without sign-in. Linking a verified Plus subscription from either store to the same DoseWeek account enables encrypted sync on both platforms. Verified Plus also shares ad removal and Plus tools across both platforms. To revoke Apple sign-in, the server may retain an Apple refresh token encrypted with a separate server key, distinct from health-record end-to-end keys.

If you turn it on separately, DoseWeek encrypts all your records on the device (injection plans and their changes, dose records, meals, body measurements, supplies and import history) together with supported settings, for automatic backup and sync across both platforms. The settings that sync are appearance (colour and light or dark mode), the weight-change unit, the body map view, whether injection reminders are on, whether reminders show details or the medication name, recording without a plan, supply tracking, and your food search market and search-language choices. When one of these is changed on another device, this device applies it and tells you once about an important change, such as reminders or supply tracking. Sync never turns on an AI feature: you turn it on yourself on each device. Permissions, connections and display choices that belong to a single device are not synced: notification permission, the app lock, health-data, calendar and cloud-storage connections, and widget appearance. When you restore from Settings, the saved settings are applied and the app tells you which settings changed. This is end-to-end encryption: the server keeps only encrypted snapshots and the account and purchase records it needs. Your data key is stored only in a form locked by your recovery code, and the recovery code stays on your devices, so the operator cannot read your health records. The server handles account and provider identifiers, verified Plus state, ciphertext, versions and minimal access metadata, but receives neither readable health records nor your recovery code. A new device needs the recovery code; if it is lost, the developer cannot recover the encrypted data. An empty device may restore after code confirmation; a device with existing records requires a preview and merge confirmation. To recheck subscription status with the store, the server also keeps keyed purchase digests and encrypted lookup references containing an Apple original transaction ID or store purchase token. References use AES-256-GCM with a server-only key separate from the end-to-end health-record keys and are used only for store checks. Account deletion erases references; expired references are pruned after 30 days. Keyed digest markers that prevent duplicate purchase linking are kept for 5 years as records of payment and supply under Article 6 of the Enforcement Decree of the Korean Act on the Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce, and are then deleted. Content you consent to and send to the AI record assistant (Pro) is the exception to end-to-end encryption: the DoseWeek server and Google can read it while an answer is being made. Google can also read it for as long as Google holds it: in memory for up to 24 hours, or for up to 90 days when Google’s abuse checks flag a request, which authorized Google staff may then review.

Health-information sync needs separate optional consent, distinct from general account processing, terms and usage analytics. OS health permissions or acknowledging the privacy policy do not replace it. We process medicine names, injection schedules and dose records, body measurements such as weight, meals and protein, symptoms and notes to continue and restore records on other devices using the same account. Records are encrypted on the device before being sent to the server. They are kept while sync is used and deleted 30 days after the last store-confirmed Plus period ends. Subscription status is checked again before deletion; a store outage may defer it by at most 7 days. Direct deletion or withdrawal of consent does not use this waiting period. Refusing consent keeps basic local-record features available; health-information sync is unavailable. Including existing device records is a separate choice.

Usage analytics and its international transfer each need separate optional consent, independent of account sign-in, sync and ad choices. Refusing either keeps analytics off and core features available. Health records and recovery codes are not sent to analytics.

A public announcement can be checked when the app opens, before onboarding. The per-announcement ‘Do not show for one week’ choice stays on this device. The announcement request may process connection data such as an IP address; the web server keeps an access log of the IP address, time, requested address, response status and size, referrer and browser or app identifier (user agent) for up to 11 days. This request also passes through Cloudflare; the privacy policy gives the overseas transfer details.

2. Apple Health (HealthKit) integration

Apple Health integration is read-only. Only the body metrics you allow — weight, BMI, body fat percentage, lean body mass, and waist circumference — are read and shown or stored on your device. You can change permissions at any time in iOS Settings. DoseWeek does not write any data to Apple Health. What it reads is kept in app-private storage and is not sent to the developer in readable form. Like your other records, it is included in your encrypted backups and, if you turn on account sync, in the end-to-end encrypted sync copy with its source and your hide choices, which the developer cannot read. Information read from Apple Health is not used for AI features either and is not sent to the AI record assistant.

3. Backups and exports

The file-backup details below describe manual export and import. Plus automatic server backup is a separate path.

The live clinical database and encrypted local backup envelope are excluded from device backups. A backup is created only when you choose to create one and is encrypted on device with AES-256-GCM. It leaves the app only when you explicitly export it through the system file picker, saving it to a location you choose in the Files app. You manage the exported ciphertext and recovery code; the developer cannot access either. The location you choose in the Files app may be outside this device — a file provider you have set up — and that provider's own terms and your device settings then also apply. A backup file saved outside the app is not removed when you delete DoseWeek's data; you have to delete it yourself. A backup made on iPhone or iPad can be restored only in DoseWeek on iPhone or iPad. DoseWeek on other platforms uses different backup formats, so backup files cannot be moved between platforms.

PDF and CSV exports are plaintext files generated on device only when you request them. They are delivered to the destination you choose in the system share sheet. After sharing, DoseWeek and the developer cannot control copies or retention; store and delete exported files securely.

While Plus is active, you can separately consent to turn on automatic server backup and sync. App records are end-to-end encrypted on the device; the server cannot read the backed-up contents. Free users can still manually export and import files and use the in-app backup reminder.

DoseWeek does not use iCloud or CloudKit to back up app data. A backup file you manually export to a cloud location you choose is a separate path governed by that provider.

4. Notifications and widgets

All notifications are local notifications scheduled directly on your device. No push servers are used; notifications never include dose amounts, and you can choose in Settings whether the medication name is shown.

Widgets you add can show a schedule countdown and status on the Home Screen, Lock Screen or StandBy. Other people who can see those surfaces may see this information. App Lock does not hide widgets; visibility on the Lock Screen depends on your device’s widget privacy settings. Widgets never show medication names or dose amounts. They are read-only: tapping a widget only opens the app and never records an administration. Widget processing stays on the device and makes no separate network transmissions.

5. Usage analytics (optional)

This section covers optional usage analytics only. Its statements about advertising IDs, Google signals and advertising personalization describe the analytics SDK. Ads in the free version use a different Google SDK with its own consent; section 11 describes them.

Acknowledging the required privacy notice does not permit analytics. Analytics is enabled only after separate consent to usage analytics and overseas transfer. Both optional choices are off by default, and analytics is disabled if either is refused or withdrawn.

Usage analytics is optional and initially off. Acknowledging the required privacy notice does not grant permission. Only after you opt in does Google Analytics for Firebase start and send automatic lifecycle and technical events, such as first opens, sessions, activity frequency, engagement time and app or operating-system updates, to Google. The developer uses usage reports to understand app use. DoseWeek adds no custom events.

Analytics can include Firebase-generated app-instance or installation identifiers, event times, app version, language, device brand, model and class, operating-system details including minor versions, and screen resolution. Google processes IP addresses for service operation and approximate location, including city; a city's derived coordinates are not your GPS location. No GPS or location permission is used for analytics. Google processes the data on its servers, potentially outside your country, and the developer can access reports. These records are not inherently anonymous.

DoseWeek does not send health records, medication names, doses, notes, typed or voice input, screen names or routes to analytics. It sets no user ID or custom user properties. Advertising IDs and IDFV collection, automatic screen reporting, Google signals and advertising personalization are disabled. Crashlytics and Performance Monitoring are not used.

Google Analytics user-level and event-level retention is set to 2 months, with reset on new activity off. Setting changes take 24 hours to apply; expired data is deleted in Google's monthly process, not exactly at the two-month instant. This setting does not govern standard aggregated reports and does not guarantee permanent availability of those reports.

Long-term storage of anonymous monthly totals is planned only after a privacy review and verified export that prevents identification. No such export has been made with real data yet; raw analytics is not treated as anonymous.

6. Deleting your data

Running Settings > Delete All Local Records immediately removes every record from DoseWeek and deletes the on-device recovery code. DoseWeek then securely cleans the private database files. If iOS temporarily prevents that cleanup, the app tells you to run the deletion again or relaunch DoseWeek and automatically retries before reopening the database. Deleted records are not restored. Manage exported files and calendar-provider copies separately. When an update first changes how DoseWeek stores data on this device, the app keeps at most one protected copy of your records as they were before that update, on this device only; later updates do not replace it. The copy is excluded from device backups, is never sent anywhere, is not shown or exported by the app, and does not expire automatically. Settings > Delete All Local Records, restoring a backup, or Remove imported Apple Health data deletes it; deleting or hiding individual records does not change it.

If you refuse either optional consent, analytics stays off and core functions remain available. You can withdraw either consent in Settings. Withdrawal stops future analytics collection but does not automatically delete records already sent to Google.

Withdrawing analytics consent stops future collection and requests a reset of local analytics data and its identifier when the SDK is already initialized. If data-deletion recovery occurs before the SDK is initialized, a local reset is requested first when you later consent again to analytics and overseas transfer and activate the SDK. This is not confirmation of Google server deletion or completed disk cleanup.

While analytics is on, the app can show you an analytics deletion request code: open Settings > Usage analytics (optional) and tap “View deletion request code”. This code contains the app-instance identifier used to locate analytics records for the current installation. If you wish, copy it before turning analytics off or deleting app data and request deletion at wonyoung@wonyoungchoi.dev. Viewing or copying the code does not send a deletion request. Do not send health records.

The code can be shown only while analytics is on. After you turn analytics off or delete app data, that code can no longer be shown or recovered. Without the previous identifier, we may be unable to locate records already sent for that installation. You can still withdraw analytics consent at any time.

The encrypted server copy is kept while Plus is active. It is deleted 30 days after the end of the last Plus period that the server verified with the App Store or another supported store. Before deleting, the server checks with the store again and keeps the copy if Plus was renewed. If the store cannot be reached, deletion may be postponed by up to 7 days. At any time you can delete only the server copy (reset sync), which keeps your account and the records on your devices, or delete the whole account. If you request direct deletion or withdraw consent, the 30-day waiting period and store-outage extension do not apply. Access stops immediately and the data covered by the request is erased from live storage without delay. Deleting only the server copy keeps the account. Completion is reported after the actual result is confirmed. Server backups are kept for at most 7 days, so deleted data leaves those backups within 7 days. Records on your devices and files you exported or stored separately, for example in a cloud storage service, must be deleted separately. Deleting the DoseWeek account does not cancel a store subscription. DoseWeek account and server-data deletion is reported separately from provider disconnection. If disconnection cannot be confirmed, you receive instructions for manual removal; no automatic retry is promised. After account deletion, the server keeps only a keyed marker of each purchase that was linked to the account, without any link to the account, so that the purchase cannot silently move to another account. This marker is kept for 5 years as a record of payment and supply under Article 6 of the Enforcement Decree of the Korean Act on the Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce, and is then deleted. Records of consumer complaints and dispute handling, such as support requests, are kept for 3 years under the same article. If you bought Plus on another supported store, the separate purchase-verification server keeps its purchase records (a one-way hash of the purchase token, the product, subscription state and times) until 90 days after the latest of the paid-period end, grace-period end and last check, and notification identifiers for 30 days; these records are not linked to a DoseWeek account and are deleted on that schedule. These purchase-verification records hold only the verification state, with no price, payment method or order details, so they are not payment records. Payment records are kept by the store that sold the subscription.

Email wonyoung@wonyoungchoi.dev to request deletion of your DoseWeek account and associated server data without reinstalling the app or subscribing to Plus. Identify the sign-in provider and, if known, your DoseWeek account ID. We explain identity verification by email. Do not send passwords, recovery codes, health records or purchase tokens. Composing or sending the email does not confirm deletion. Account/server-data erasure and provider disconnection outcomes are confirmed and communicated separately. If disconnection cannot be confirmed, we explain manual removal in the provider’s account settings. Canceling a subscription is not a condition of this request; manage store billing separately.

Settings > Delete All Local Records also deletes Plus tool data: meal templates, visit report settings and the Today layout. It does not cancel a Plus subscription, which you manage in the App Store. It keeps the ad-frequency counters and the short subscription cache on the device, so deleting records does not reset ad limits; neither contains health data.

Destruction procedure and method: when you delete your account in the app (Settings > Account > Delete account, confirmed with your Apple or Google sign-in), the server deletes in the same request your sign-in links and sessions, the purchase links of the account, your AI record assistant consent record and usage counts, the account record, and the file that holds your encrypted sync copy. Deleting only the server copy (reset sync) deletes that encrypted file and keeps the account. Without a request, the server checks every hour and deletes the encrypted copy 30 days after the last store-confirmed Plus period, as described above. These records exist only in electronic form: the entry is removed from the server’s data file, which is then rewritten, and the encrypted copy file is deleted from the server’s storage. Scheduled server backups are not running at present; when they are enabled, each encrypted backup is deleted within 7 days of its creation, so a deleted account disappears from the backups within that time. Web server access logs are deleted after at most 11 days, and requests to the account, sync and AI functions are not written to an access log. Records that a law requires us to keep are kept only for the stated period (the purchase marker described above, which has no link to your account, for 5 years, and correspondence about complaints or disputes for 3 years) and the operator deletes them when that period ends.

8. On-device AI

Optional AI entry and Visit Prep use only Apple Foundation Models' on-device Apple Intelligence model, SystemLanguageModel.default. AI entry input and structured model output are processed in a temporary in-memory session on the device. DoseWeek does not send them off device or persist the temporary session or output before you confirm. If you continue, your original sentence becomes an editable draft note, and only the fields and note you review and explicitly confirm are stored in the local clinical database. For Visit Prep, the model receives only closed fact identifiers, not clinical values, and returns only order and reviewed-expression enums. The app builds every sentence from localized templates and computed values. The resulting summary is memory-only and is not stored; it is included in a PDF only when you choose to include it. These two on-device features use no Private Cloud Compute, server model, or third-party model. You can turn these features off in Settings.

Optional voice input starts only after your action and microphone permission. Speech is processed on device; required Apple speech resources are downloaded only with your agreement. Audio is not saved or exported. Cancellation, locking or backgrounding stops recording. Review the final transcript before continuing to a record draft. Unsupported devices or languages retain typed input.

This section covers only AI recording and visit preparation, which are processed on the device. The AI record assistant (Pro), which is processed on a server, is described separately under “AI record assistant (Pro)” in this policy.

9. Optional calendar sync

Optional calendar sync (off by default): if you turn it on, DoseWeek writes a title (“Personal reminder” unless you choose “Injection day”) and a time for each upcoming injection day to the calendar you select, and updates or removes those events automatically. The events are stored by that calendar's account (for example iCloud, Google or Exchange), may sync to your other devices and to anyone the calendar is shared with, and are not sent to the developer. Turning sync off lets you remove upcoming events. Deleting DoseWeek data removes them when calendar access still allows it. Copies already synced by the calendar provider follow that provider.

10. Meal records and backup format

Meal records: the meal name, date, optional time and meal kind you enter, and the energy, protein, carbohydrate and fat totals you know, are kept in app-private storage on the device. A value you leave blank stays unknown and is not calculated. Meal records are included in the encrypted backup, can be written to a plaintext CSV file only when you ask for it, and are not part of the Visit Prep view or the PDF export. The developer receives no meal information in readable form; if you turn on account sync, meal records are sent to the server only end-to-end encrypted, so the developer cannot read them. Content you consent to and send to the AI record assistant (Pro) is the exception to end-to-end encryption: the DoseWeek server and Google can read it while an answer is being made. Google can also read it for as long as Google holds it: in memory for up to 24 hours, or for up to 90 days when Google’s abuse checks flag a request, which authorized Google staff may then review.

Backup format: the new features raise the format numbers of the app database and of the encrypted backup file. Backups written by earlier versions can still be restored, but a backup written by the new version cannot be restored by an earlier version. Calendar event links stay on the device, are not included in a backup, and are kept when you restore on the same device. Unsaved drafts are not included either.

Selected food labels are read on device. DoseWeek uses only the image you choose, without full-library permission. It does not keep the original image or location metadata or include images in exports or backups. Closing the review, locking or backgrounding discards the temporary review. The original photo is unchanged. Recognized values require confirmation and are never saved automatically.

11. Ads in the free version

This section applies to versions of DoseWeek that are a free download with the optional Plus subscription. Free users see ads; Plus subscribers do not. Ads are shown by the Google Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob), and ad consent is handled by Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP). Both are provided by Google. No ad is shown until the App Store has confirmed whether you have Plus. Once Plus is confirmed, the app removes ads at once and does not start the ad SDK; ad requests already sent before that moment are not recalled.

DoseWeek requests only non-personalized ads. The app never shows Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt, so the SDK cannot read your device's advertising identifier (IDFA), and every ad request asks for non-personalized ads.

Non-personalized ads still involve processing. When the SDK requests, shows and measures an ad, Google receives your IP address, device and app information (such as device model, operating-system version, app version and language), ad interaction data (such as views and taps), diagnostic data and identifiers the SDK creates for this app. Google uses this information to serve ads, to limit how often an ad is shown, to prevent fraud and abuse and to measure ads. Google receives it as a recipient under its own terms and privacy policy, processes it on its servers, which can be outside your country, and keeps it for the periods Google sets; DoseWeek cannot shorten them. The data leaves the device over the internet while ads are requested, shown and measured. The developer sees only aggregated AdMob reports, such as ad views and earnings.

Where the law requires consent, for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland and some US states, Google's consent message appears before any ad is requested, and ads are requested only after the SDK reports that they may be. To find out whether a message is needed, the app asks Google's consent service at launch, for Free users only; this request also reaches Google and is separate from ad requests. You can review or change your choice at any time in Settings > Privacy choices for ads; where your region needs no choice, that screen says so. The one-time notice that DoseWeek is now free with ads only informs you; reading it is not consent. If ad processing is not allowed, because you declined, the form could not load or the consent service is unavailable, DoseWeek shows no ads and every Free feature keeps working.

DoseWeek never passes health records, notes, medication names, doses, dates, meals or screen names to the ad SDK, and ad requests carry no keywords, content links or custom targeting. The app's ad code does not read your records. Like any SDK, Google's SDKs run inside the app process with the same technical access to the app's files as the app itself. DoseWeek does not give them your records, but it cannot technically prevent that access, so this boundary depends on Google's SDKs behaving as Google documents them.

AdMob is not linked to Firebase or Google Analytics. Ad consent and analytics consent (section 5) are separate: neither choice grants or withdraws the other, and the analytics settings keep advertising storage, ad user data and ad personalization denied. Counters that pace ads and the 24-hour ad-free pass stay on the device (section 12).

To report an ad, tap the AdChoices icon on the ad, or use Report an ad in Settings, which opens an email to support with the time and the screen where the ad appeared. It adds no health data; please do not add any.

Ad data can be transferred outside your country. The details of this transfer are below.

If your choice does not allow ad processing, DoseWeek requests no ads and every Free feature keeps working; the consent check at launch still contacts Google.
