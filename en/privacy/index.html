DoseWeek · iOS 1.0.5

Privacy Policy

DoseWeek is a personal record-keeping tool for injection tracking. This policy explains what information the app handles, where it goes and what you can choose, including ads in the free version and Plus purchases.

1. Where your data is stored

Health records, including schedules, administrations, notes and body measurements, stay on your device by default. The developer does not receive these records. Exports, backups and optional integrations are described separately. Optional usage analytics follows the rules below.

2. Apple Health (HealthKit) integration

Apple Health integration is read-only. Only the body metrics you allow — weight, BMI, body fat percentage, lean body mass, and waist circumference — are read and shown or stored on your device. You can change permissions at any time in iOS Settings. DoseWeek does not write any data to Apple Health.

3. Backups and exports

The live clinical database and encrypted local backup envelope are excluded from device backups. A backup is created only when you choose to create one and is encrypted on device with AES-256-GCM. It leaves the app only when you explicitly export it through the system file picker, saving it to a location you choose in the Files app. You manage the exported ciphertext and recovery code; the developer cannot access either. The location you choose in the Files app may be outside this device — a file provider you have set up — and that provider's own terms and your device settings then also apply. A backup file saved outside the app is not removed when you delete DoseWeek's data; you have to delete it yourself. A backup made on iPhone or iPad can be restored only in DoseWeek on iPhone or iPad. DoseWeek on other platforms uses different backup formats, so backup files cannot be moved between platforms.

PDF and CSV exports are plaintext files generated on device only when you request them. They are delivered to the destination you choose in the system share sheet. After sharing, DoseWeek and the developer cannot control copies or retention; store and delete exported files securely.

4. Notifications and widgets

All notifications are local notifications scheduled directly on your device. No push servers are used; notifications never include dose amounts, and you can choose in Settings whether the medication name is shown.

Widgets you add can show a schedule countdown and status on the Home Screen, Lock Screen or StandBy. Other people who can see those surfaces may see this information. App Lock does not hide widgets; visibility on the Lock Screen depends on your device’s widget privacy settings. Widgets never show medication names or dose amounts. They are read-only: tapping a widget only opens the app and never records an administration. Widget processing stays on the device and makes no separate network transmissions.

5. Usage analytics (optional)

This section covers optional usage analytics only. Its statements about advertising IDs, Google signals and advertising personalization describe the analytics SDK. Ads in the free version use a different Google SDK with its own consent; section 11 describes them.

Acknowledging the required privacy notice does not permit analytics. Analytics is enabled only after separate consent to usage analytics and overseas transfer. Both optional choices are off by default, and analytics is disabled if either is refused or withdrawn.

Usage analytics is optional and initially off. Acknowledging the required privacy notice does not grant permission. Only after you opt in does Google Analytics for Firebase start and send automatic lifecycle and technical events, such as first opens, sessions, activity frequency, engagement time and app or operating-system updates, to Google. The developer uses usage reports to understand app use. DoseWeek adds no custom events.

Analytics can include Firebase-generated app-instance or installation identifiers, event times, app version, language, device brand, model and class, operating-system details including minor versions, and screen resolution. Google processes IP addresses for service operation and approximate location, including city; a city's derived coordinates are not your GPS location. No GPS or location permission is used for analytics. Google processes the data on its servers, potentially outside your country, and the developer can access reports. These records are not inherently anonymous.

DoseWeek does not send health records, medication names, doses, notes, typed or voice input, screen names or routes to analytics. It sets no user ID or custom user properties. Advertising IDs and IDFV collection, automatic screen reporting, Google signals and advertising personalization are disabled. Crashlytics and Performance Monitoring are not used.

Google Analytics user-level and event-level retention is set to 2 months, with reset on new activity off. Setting changes take 24 hours to apply; expired data is deleted in Google's monthly process, not exactly at the two-month instant. This setting does not govern standard aggregated reports and does not guarantee permanent availability of those reports.

Long-term storage of anonymous monthly totals is planned only after a privacy review and verified export that prevents identification. No such export has been made with real data yet; raw analytics is not treated as anonymous.

6. Deleting your data

Running Settings > Delete All Local Records immediately removes every record from DoseWeek and deletes the on-device recovery code. DoseWeek then securely cleans the private database files. If iOS temporarily prevents that cleanup, the app tells you to run the deletion again or relaunch DoseWeek and automatically retries before reopening the database. Deleted records are not restored. Manage exported files and calendar-provider copies separately. When an update first changes how DoseWeek stores data on this device, the app keeps at most one protected copy of your records as they were before that update, on this device only; later updates do not replace it. The copy is excluded from device backups, is never sent anywhere, is not shown or exported by the app, and does not expire automatically. Settings > Delete All Local Records, restoring a backup, or Remove imported Apple Health data deletes it; deleting or hiding individual records does not change it.

If you refuse either optional consent, analytics stays off and core functions remain available. You can withdraw either consent in Settings. Withdrawal stops future analytics collection but does not automatically delete records already sent to Google.

Withdrawing analytics consent stops future collection and requests a reset of local analytics data and its identifier when the SDK is already initialized. If data-deletion recovery occurs before the SDK is initialized, a local reset is requested first when you later consent again to analytics and overseas transfer and activate the SDK. This is not confirmation of Google server deletion or completed disk cleanup.

While analytics is on, the app can show you an analytics deletion request code: open Settings > Usage analytics (optional) and tap “View deletion request code”. This code contains the app-instance identifier used to locate analytics records for the current installation. If you wish, copy it before turning analytics off or deleting app data and request deletion at wonyoung@wonyoungchoi.dev. Viewing or copying the code does not send a deletion request. Do not send health records.

The code can be shown only while analytics is on. After you turn analytics off or delete app data, that code can no longer be shown or recovered. Without the previous identifier, we may be unable to locate records already sent for that installation. You can still withdraw analytics consent at any time.

Settings > Delete All Local Records also deletes Plus tool data: meal templates, visit report settings and the Today layout. It does not cancel a Plus subscription, which you manage in the App Store. It keeps the ad-frequency counters and the short subscription cache on the device, so deleting records does not reset ad limits; neither contains health data.

8. On-device AI

Optional AI entry and Visit Prep use only Apple Foundation Models' on-device Apple Intelligence model, SystemLanguageModel.default. AI entry input and structured model output are processed in a temporary in-memory session on the device. DoseWeek does not send them off device or persist the temporary session or output before you confirm. If you continue, your original sentence becomes an editable draft note, and only the fields and note you review and explicitly confirm are stored in the local clinical database. For Visit Prep, the model receives only closed fact identifiers, not clinical values, and returns only order and reviewed-expression enums. The app builds every sentence from localized templates and computed values. The resulting summary is memory-only and is not stored; it is included in a PDF only when you choose to include it. No Private Cloud Compute, server model, or third-party model is used. You can turn these features off in Settings.

Optional voice input starts only after your action and microphone permission. Speech is processed on device; required Apple speech resources are downloaded only with your agreement. Audio is not saved or exported. Cancellation, locking or backgrounding stops recording. Review the final transcript before continuing to a record draft. Unsupported devices or languages retain typed input.

9. Optional calendar sync

Optional calendar sync (off by default): if you turn it on, DoseWeek writes a title (“Personal reminder” unless you choose “Injection day”) and a time for each upcoming injection day to the calendar you select, and updates or removes those events automatically. The events are stored by that calendar's account (for example iCloud, Google or Exchange), may sync to your other devices and to anyone the calendar is shared with, and are not sent to the developer. Turning sync off lets you remove upcoming events. Deleting DoseWeek data removes them when calendar access still allows it. Copies already synced by the calendar provider follow that provider.

10. Meal records and backup format

Meal records: the meal name, date, optional time and meal kind you enter, and the energy, protein, carbohydrate and fat totals you know, are kept in app-private storage on the device. A value you leave blank stays unknown and is not calculated. Meal records are included in the encrypted backup, can be written to a plaintext CSV file only when you ask for it, and are not part of the Visit Prep view or the PDF export. The developer receives no meal information.

Backup format: the new features raise the format numbers of the app database and of the encrypted backup file. Backups written by earlier versions can still be restored, but a backup written by the new version cannot be restored by an earlier version. Calendar event links stay on the device, are not included in a backup, and are kept when you restore on the same device. Unsaved drafts are not included either.

Selected food labels are read on device. DoseWeek uses only the image you choose, without full-library permission. It does not keep the original image or location metadata or include images in exports or backups. Closing the review, locking or backgrounding discards the temporary review. The original photo is unchanged. Recognized values require confirmation and are never saved automatically.

11. Ads in the free version

This section applies to versions of DoseWeek that are a free download with the optional Plus subscription. Free users see ads; Plus subscribers do not. Ads are shown by the Google Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob), and ad consent is handled by Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP). Both are provided by Google. No ad is shown until the App Store has confirmed whether you have Plus. Once Plus is confirmed, the app removes ads at once and does not start the ad SDK; ad requests already sent before that moment are not recalled.

DoseWeek requests only non-personalized ads. The app never shows Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt, so the SDK cannot read your device's advertising identifier (IDFA), and every ad request asks for non-personalized ads.

Non-personalized ads still involve processing. When the SDK requests, shows and measures an ad, Google receives your IP address, device and app information (such as device model, operating-system version, app version and language), ad interaction data (such as views and taps), diagnostic data and identifiers the SDK creates for this app. Google uses this information to serve ads, to limit how often an ad is shown, to prevent fraud and abuse and to measure ads. Google receives it as a recipient under its own terms and privacy policy, processes it on its servers, which can be outside your country, and keeps it for the periods Google sets; DoseWeek cannot shorten them. The data leaves the device over the internet while ads are requested, shown and measured. The developer sees only aggregated AdMob reports, such as ad views and earnings.

Where the law requires consent, for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland and some US states, Google's consent message appears before any ad is requested, and ads are requested only after the SDK reports that they may be. To find out whether a message is needed, the app asks Google's consent service at launch, for Free users only; this request also reaches Google and is separate from ad requests. You can review or change your choice at any time in Settings > Privacy choices for ads; where your region needs no choice, that screen says so. The one-time notice that DoseWeek is now free with ads only informs you; reading it is not consent. If ad processing is not allowed, because you declined, the form could not load or the consent service is unavailable, DoseWeek shows no ads and every Free feature keeps working.

DoseWeek never passes health records, notes, medication names, doses, dates, meals or screen names to the ad SDK, and ad requests carry no keywords, content links or custom targeting. The app's ad code does not read your records. Like any SDK, Google's SDKs run inside the app process with the same technical access to the app's files as the app itself. DoseWeek does not give them your records, but it cannot technically prevent that access, so this boundary depends on Google's SDKs behaving as Google documents them.

AdMob is not linked to Firebase or Google Analytics. Ad consent and analytics consent (section 5) are separate: neither choice grants or withdraws the other, and the analytics settings keep advertising storage, ad user data and ad personalization denied. Counters that pace ads and the 24-hour ad-free pass stay on the device (section 12).

To report an ad, tap the AdChoices icon on the ad, or use Report an ad in Settings, which opens an email to support with the time and the screen where the ad appeared. It adds no health data; please do not add any.

12. Plus subscription and purchases

Plus is an optional auto-renewing subscription, monthly or annual, sold through the App Store. The price and billing period are shown in the app and on the App Store before you buy. Apple processes the payment and your Apple Account under its own terms; DoseWeek does not receive your payment details.

To unlock Plus, the app reads the App Store's signed transaction and renewal information on the device and unlocks Plus only when that information verifies. In this version the iOS app sends no purchase data to the developer. Apple can send the developer's purchase-verification server notices about the subscription, such as a renewal, an expiry or a refund; these identify a transaction, not you by name or email address, and the server keeps only hashed transaction identifiers, the product, subscription state, relevant times and notice identifiers, with no health data. The server location and operator details are listed here before this version is released. How long the server keeps these records is also listed here before this version is released.

The app keeps a short cache of the last verified subscription state (state, plan, verification time and the end of the paid or grace period), so Plus keeps working offline for a limited time, at most seven days after the last verification. It also keeps ad-frequency counters, the time of the last Plus offer and the 24-hour ad-free pass. These stay in the app's preferences on the device, are not health data, are not included in DoseWeek's encrypted backup and are not sent anywhere by DoseWeek.

Plus tools keep their data on the device: meal templates, visit report settings and the Today layout. This data stays in a protected app-private file that is excluded from device backups and, in this version, is not part of DoseWeek's encrypted backup. Period summaries are calculated on the device from your records. If Plus ends, this data is kept.

Manage, cancel or change your plan in your Apple Account's subscription settings, which you can open from Settings in DoseWeek. Cancelling stops the next renewal; Plus stays on until the end of the period you paid for. Restore purchases in Settings asks the App Store for your current purchases. Deleting the app or its data does not cancel a subscription.

Buying DoseWeek when it was a paid app does not create a Plus subscription, and nothing is charged automatically. The app does not read the original purchase, the app receipt or the install date to decide about Plus or ads.

The app also stores recent reward grant times, the ad-free expiry time and the latest device time it has observed. It uses these records to limit rewards to two in any rolling 24 hours and pause the pass when the clock moves back more than five minutes.

Processing purposes, consent and safeguards

DoseWeek processes medication names, schedules and administration records, body measurements, meals and protein, symptoms and notes to keep the records you request. Health records stay on the device by default. Optional account and encrypted sync handling, including identifiers, purchase verification and server retention, is explained in the account/sync disclosure. General account processing relies on providing the requested service; optional health sync requires separate explicit health-data consent. OS permission, agreement to Terms and acknowledgement of this policy do not replace that consent. Analytics and any required overseas-transfer consent remain separate and optional.

Health-platform access is optional and read-only, limited to the metrics listed in the health section. DoseWeek requests no health-platform writing or deletion permission. You can revoke access in device settings and continue manual entry. DoseWeek does not publicly disclose or sell recorded health content, including medication and body-measurement records, share that content for advertising, or use it to select ads. Health content is excluded from analytics events, screen names and logs sent to advertising or analytics services. User-chosen exports, calendars and file providers remain under your control and those recipients' policies.

Access controls, device locks, encrypted backup envelopes, encrypted transport and separate recovery keys protect the data within their stated scope. Encryption does not make account identifiers, IP addresses or health-revealing metadata anonymous. Records are retained for the purposes and periods described in the retention and account/sync sections. After deletion, data is made unavailable and removed without undue delay; encrypted backup copies must be removed within 7 days and must not reappear as active records after restoration. Legally required transaction records need a disclosed item, legal basis and period before collection; unresolved purchase-digest retention is a release blocker.

Data incidents and breach response

Report a suspected privacy incident to wonyoung@wonyoungchoi.dev without sending health records, passwords or recovery codes. We assess intrusions and unauthorized SDK disclosures, contain exposure, preserve evidence and identify affected data and recipients. Encryption is assessed together with key protection and account or health-revealing metadata; it is not an automatic exemption.

In Korea, affected individuals are notified without undue delay and normally within 72 hours of learning of a leak, with any permitted exception documented. PIPA reporting to PIPC or KISA is due within 72 hours where at least 1,000 people are affected, sensitive or unique-identification data is involved, or qualifying external unauthorized access caused the leak. These triggers are independent; a small health-data incident can still require reporting.

Where the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule applies, individuals are notified without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 calendar days after discovery. For 500 or more affected people, the FTC is notified at the same time as individuals; for fewer than 500, within 60 calendar days after the end of the calendar year. If 500 or more affected residents are in one state or jurisdiction, required media notice is also given. Required electronic and substitute notices follow the Rule. Where GDPR applies, qualifying authority notice is within 72 hours of awareness unless risk is unlikely, and high-risk individuals are informed without undue delay.
