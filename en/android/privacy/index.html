Privacy Policy for DoseWeek on Android

This text takes effect on October 3, 2026. The parts about accounts, sync, Plus, ads and other new features describe the first DoseWeek version that has them (1.0.6) and apply from the release of that version. Earlier versions do not have these features. This policy applies to DoseWeek 1.0.0 (versionCode 12) and later on Android, including the current version, 1.0.6.

1. Scope and responsible parties

DoseWeek and this Privacy Policy are run by Wonyoung Labs, a sole proprietorship based in the Republic of Korea. For questions about data handling, contact wonyoung@wonyoungchoi.dev.

DoseWeek for Android is for people aged 18 or older. It does not ask for or transmit a date of birth or numerical age; only a versioned eligibility choice is stored on the device. If you decline or do not confirm eligibility, ordinary app functions and AI are unavailable. Existing local records are not deleted; only export of an encrypted backup file and recovery code is available after authentication when App Lock is enabled. Usage analytics, overseas transfer and AI feature consents are separate.

You may optionally sign in to a DoseWeek account with Apple or Google. Existing local-record features remain available without sign-in. Linking a verified Plus subscription from either store to the same DoseWeek account enables encrypted sync on both platforms. Verified Plus also shares ad removal and Plus tools across both platforms. To revoke Apple sign-in, the server may retain an Apple refresh token encrypted with a separate server key, distinct from health-record end-to-end keys.

If you turn it on separately, DoseWeek encrypts all your records on the device (injection plans and their changes, dose records, meals, body measurements, supplies and import history) together with supported settings, for automatic backup and sync across both platforms. The settings that sync are appearance (colour and light or dark mode), the weight-change unit, the body map view, whether injection reminders are on, whether reminders show details or the medication name, recording without a plan, supply tracking, and your food search market and search-language choices. When one of these is changed on another device, this device applies it and tells you once about an important change, such as reminders or supply tracking. Sync never turns on an AI feature: you turn it on yourself on each device. Permissions, connections and display choices that belong to a single device are not synced: notification permission, the app lock, health-data, calendar and cloud-storage connections, and widget appearance. When you restore from Settings, the saved settings are applied and the app tells you which settings changed. This is end-to-end encryption: the server keeps only encrypted snapshots and the account and purchase records it needs. Your data key is stored only in a form locked by your recovery code, and the recovery code stays on your devices, so the operator cannot read your health records. The server handles account and provider identifiers, verified Plus state, ciphertext, versions and minimal access metadata, but receives neither readable health records nor your recovery code. A new device needs the recovery code; if it is lost, the developer cannot recover the encrypted data. An empty device may restore after code confirmation; a device with existing records requires a preview and merge confirmation. To recheck subscription status with the store, the server also keeps keyed purchase digests and encrypted lookup references containing an Apple original transaction ID or store purchase token. References use AES-256-GCM with a server-only key separate from the end-to-end health-record keys and are used only for store checks. Account deletion erases references; expired references are pruned after 30 days. Keyed digest markers that prevent duplicate purchase linking are kept for 5 years as records of payment and supply under Article 6 of the Enforcement Decree of the Korean Act on the Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce, and are then deleted. Content you consent to and send to the AI record assistant (Pro) is the exception to end-to-end encryption: the DoseWeek server and Google can read it while an answer is being made. Google can also read it for as long as Google holds it: in memory for up to 24 hours, or for up to 90 days when Google’s abuse checks flag a request, which authorized Google staff may then review.

Health-information sync needs separate optional consent, distinct from general account processing, terms and usage analytics. OS health permissions or acknowledging the privacy policy do not replace it. We process medicine names, injection schedules and dose records, body measurements such as weight, meals and protein, symptoms and notes to continue and restore records on other devices using the same account. Records are encrypted on the device before being sent to the server. They are kept while sync is used and deleted 30 days after the last store-confirmed Plus period ends. Subscription status is checked again before deletion; a store outage may defer it by at most 7 days. Direct deletion or withdrawal of consent does not use this waiting period. Refusing consent keeps basic local-record features available; health-information sync is unavailable. Including existing device records is a separate choice.

A public announcement can be checked when the app opens, before onboarding. The per-announcement ‘Do not show for one week’ choice stays on this device. The announcement request may process connection data such as an IP address; the web server keeps an access log of the IP address, time, requested address, response status and size, referrer and browser or app identifier (user agent) for up to 11 days. This request also passes through Cloudflare; the privacy policy gives the overseas transfer details.

2. Information you store on your device

DoseWeek keeps the following information that you manually enter and confirm in app-private storage. Apart from the optional integrations, backups and exports described in this policy, it is processed only on your Android device to provide the app's features.

  • Weekly injection schedule, including product and region option, dose, weekday, time, and time-zone basis
  • Actual administration records, including date, time, dose, selected injection site, and optional note
  • Weekly schedule entries that you mark as skipped
  • Inventory count managed as remaining administrations
  • Weight and waist circumference that you enter manually
  • Side-effect logs, including symptom, severity, time, and optional note, and the change history of edited or deleted administration records, including the reason you enter
  • Settings you choose: accent color and appearance, reminder options including whether reminder text names your medication, and whether the app lock is on

4. Storage, backup, and device transfer

The file-backup details below describe manual export and import. Plus automatic server backup is a separate path.

You can manually save all app data to an encrypted backup file at a location you choose through Android's system file picker (SAF), and restore by selecting that file. This process needs no DoseWeek account and no broad storage permission, and it uses no network access. Backups can be restored only in DoseWeek for Android. DoseWeek on other platforms uses a different backup format, so backups cannot be moved between platforms.

If you select an external or cloud document provider, that provider stores the backup under its own privacy policy and terms. The DoseWeek developer receives neither the backup file nor its recovery code.

The backup uses AES-256-GCM authenticated encryption and a separate high-entropy recovery code. It is not a DoseWeek-operated cloud backup, and there is no code-reset service. Keep the file and code safe and separate. If the code is lost, the developer and support cannot reset it or decrypt the file. Until you confirm that you saved a file backup's recovery code, DoseWeek keeps that code on this device only, wrapped with a key held by the device, so that you can view it again; it is deleted once you confirm.

During restore, DoseWeek decrypts and completely validates the file, including its structure and data relationships, before atomically replacing current app data in one local transaction.

As an option, DoseWeek can also keep that encrypted backup in your own Google Drive app-data folder. Drive can see a file name containing a random identifier, the file size, the creation time, the payload version, the key version, and a random snapshot identifier. No medication name, dose, measurement, date of treatment, or note appears outside the encrypted envelope.

Encryption happens on the device before upload, with the same AES-256-GCM envelope the offline file export uses. The recovery code is never stored with the backup and is never sent anywhere. Until you confirm that you saved it, you can show the same code again; after that, DoseWeek does not show it again. You must re-enter it before any automatic backup can start. If it is lost, the developer cannot read or recover the backup.

Optional automatic backup is off by default; when it is on it runs about once a day when the device allows it, and does not promise an exact time. Deleting a backup from Drive is permanent, because Drive offers no trash for app-data files. Disconnecting stops future uploads: it does not delete backups already in Drive and does not delete records on the device, and destroying this device's copy of the recovery code is a separate, clearly labelled choice. Each backup that uploads changes, whether automatic or Back up now, is stored as a separate encrypted file. Earlier files stay until you delete them from Stored backups in the app or remove the app's data from your Google account, and they use your Google storage.

While Plus is active, you can separately consent to turn on automatic server backup and sync. App records are end-to-end encrypted on the device; the server cannot read the backed-up contents. Free users can still manually export and import files and use the in-app backup reminder.

Android OS Google Drive Auto Backup includes DoseWeek app data only while Plus is verified and active; it then backs up the database, keys and records together as one complete set. If Plus is not active or cannot be verified, for example offline or when the store check fails, DoseWeek skips that backup run instead of sending an empty or partial backup, so the previous backup stays as it is. DoseWeek does not delete existing Drive backups, and you can still restore an existing backup. This backup is kept in your Google account under Google's rules, separately from DoseWeek server copies; you can manage or delete it in your Google account's backup settings.

5. Ads in the free version

This section applies to versions of DoseWeek that are a free download with the optional Plus subscription. Free users see ads; Plus subscribers do not. Ads are shown by the Google Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob), and ad consent is handled by Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP). Both are provided by Google. No ad is shown until Google Play has confirmed whether you have Plus. Once Plus is confirmed, the app removes ads at once and does not start the ad SDK; ad requests already sent before that moment are not recalled.

DoseWeek requests only non-personalized ads. The app removes the advertising ID permission and the Android ad-services permissions for the advertising ID, attribution and topics, and it removes the install referrer service, so the SDK reads the advertising ID as zeros. Every ad request asks for non-personalized ads.

Non-personalized ads still involve processing. When the SDK requests, shows and measures an ad, Google receives your IP address, device and app information (such as device model, operating-system version, app version and language), ad interaction data (such as views and taps), diagnostic data, identifiers the SDK creates for this app, and the app set ID, which Google Play services provides and which apps from the same developer share on the device. Google uses this information to serve ads, to limit how often an ad is shown, to prevent fraud and abuse and to measure ads. Google receives it as a recipient under its own terms and privacy policy, processes it on its servers, which can be outside your country, and keeps it for the periods Google sets; DoseWeek cannot shorten them. The data leaves the device over the internet while ads are requested, shown and measured. The developer sees only aggregated AdMob reports, such as ad views and earnings.

Where the law requires consent, for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland and some US states, Google's consent message appears before any ad is requested, and ads are requested only after the SDK reports that they may be. To find out whether a message is needed, the app asks Google's consent service at launch, for Free users only; this request also reaches Google and is separate from ad requests. You can review or change your choice at any time in Settings > Privacy choices for ads; where your region needs no choice, that screen says so. The one-time notice that DoseWeek is now free with ads only informs you; reading it is not consent. If ad processing is not allowed, because you declined, the form could not load or the consent service is unavailable, DoseWeek shows no ads and every Free feature keeps working. DoseWeek for Android is still only for people aged 18 or older; the age confirmation is unchanged and separate from ad consent, usage analytics and overseas transfer.

DoseWeek never passes health records, notes, medication names, doses, dates, meals or screen names to the ad SDK, and ad requests carry no keywords, content links or custom targeting. The app's ad code does not read your records. Like any SDK, Google's SDKs run inside the app process with the same technical access to the app's files as the app itself. DoseWeek does not give them your records, but it cannot technically prevent that access, so this boundary depends on Google's SDKs behaving as Google documents them.

AdMob is not linked to Firebase or Google Analytics. Ad consent and analytics consent (section 3) are separate: neither choice grants or withdraws the other, and the analytics settings keep advertising storage, ad user data and ad personalization denied. To report an ad, tap the AdChoices icon on the ad, or use Report an ad in Settings, which opens an email to support with the time and the screen where the ad appeared. It adds no health data; please do not add any.

Ad data can be transferred outside your country. The details of this transfer are below.

Recipient: Google LLC, 1600 Amphitheatre Parkway, Mountain View, CA 94043, USA. For people in the European Economic Area and Switzerland, Google's terms name Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland. Google's privacy policy explains how to contact Google, including its data protection office: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Countries: the United States and other countries where Google operates servers. Google's data-centre locations: https://datacenters.google/locations/

Items: your IP address, device and app information, ad interaction data, diagnostic data, identifiers the SDK creates for this app, and the app set ID, as described above. For the consent check, Google receives your IP address, which it uses to work out your region, and app and device information.

When and how: at launch for the consent check (Free users only) and each time an ad is requested, shown or measured, over encrypted internet connections.

Purpose: serving ads, limiting how often an ad is shown, preventing fraud and abuse and measuring ads, and, for the consent check, finding out whether a consent message is needed and applying your choice. Google also uses such data for its own purposes, which it describes here: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

Retention: Google keeps the data for the periods set in its policies; DoseWeek cannot shorten them.

How to refuse: where Google's consent message applies, you can make or change your choice at any time in Settings > Privacy choices for ads. If your choice does not allow ad processing, DoseWeek requests no ads and every Free feature keeps working; the consent check at launch still contacts Google. Where no consent message applies, for example in Korea, Google's consent service offers no way to refuse and DoseWeek does not offer one either, so the only way to use DoseWeek without this ad processing is Plus: once Plus is confirmed, the app requests no ads and makes no consent check.

7. Retention and deletion

The encrypted server copy is kept while Plus is active. It is deleted 30 days after the end of the last Plus period that the server verified with the App Store or Google Play. Before deleting, the server checks with the store again and keeps the copy if Plus was renewed. If the store cannot be reached, deletion may be postponed by up to 7 days. At any time you can delete only the server copy (reset sync), which keeps your account and the records on your devices, or delete the whole account. If you request direct deletion or withdraw consent, the 30-day waiting period and store-outage extension do not apply. Access stops immediately and the data covered by the request is erased from live storage without delay. Deleting only the server copy keeps the account. Completion is reported after the actual result is confirmed. Server backups are kept for at most 7 days, so deleted data leaves those backups within 7 days. Records on your devices and files you exported or stored separately, for example in a cloud storage service, must be deleted separately. Deleting the DoseWeek account does not cancel a store subscription. DoseWeek account and server-data deletion is reported separately from provider disconnection. If disconnection cannot be confirmed, you receive instructions for manual removal; no automatic retry is promised. After account deletion, the server keeps only a keyed marker of each purchase that was linked to the account, without any link to the account, so that the purchase cannot silently move to another account. This marker is kept for 5 years as a record of payment and supply under Article 6 of the Enforcement Decree of the Korean Act on the Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce, and is then deleted. Records of consumer complaints and dispute handling, such as support requests, are kept for 3 years under the same article. If you bought Plus on Google Play, the separate purchase-verification server keeps its purchase records (a one-way hash of the purchase token, the product, subscription state and times) until 90 days after the latest of the paid-period end, grace-period end and last check, and notification identifiers for 30 days; these records are not linked to a DoseWeek account and are deleted on that schedule. These purchase-verification records hold only the verification state, with no price, payment method or order details, so they are not payment records. Payment records are kept by the store that sold the subscription.

Data incidents and breach response

Report a suspected privacy incident to wonyoung@wonyoungchoi.dev without sending health records, passwords or recovery codes. We assess intrusions and unauthorized SDK disclosures, contain exposure, preserve evidence and identify affected data and recipients. Encryption is assessed together with key protection and account or health-revealing metadata; it is not an automatic exemption.

In Korea, affected individuals are notified without undue delay and normally within 72 hours of learning of a leak, with any permitted exception documented. PIPA reporting to PIPC or KISA is due within 72 hours where at least 1,000 people are affected, sensitive or unique-identification data is involved, or qualifying external unauthorized access caused the leak. These triggers are independent; a small health-data incident can still require reporting.

Where the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule applies, individuals are notified without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 calendar days after discovery. For 500 or more affected people, the FTC is notified at the same time as individuals; for fewer than 500, within 60 calendar days after the end of the calendar year. If 500 or more affected residents are in one state or jurisdiction, required media notice is also given. Required electronic and substitute notices follow the Rule. Where GDPR applies, qualifying authority notice is within 72 hours of awareness unless risk is unlikely, and high-risk individuals are informed without undue delay.
