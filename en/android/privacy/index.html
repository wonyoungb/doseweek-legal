1. Scope and responsible parties

DoseWeek and this Privacy Policy are run by Wonyoung Choi, an individual developer based in the Republic of Korea. For questions about data handling, contact wonyoung@wonyoungchoi.dev.

DoseWeek for Android is for people aged 18 or older. It does not ask for or transmit a date of birth or numerical age; only a versioned eligibility choice is stored on the device. If you decline or do not confirm eligibility, ordinary app functions and AI are unavailable. Existing local records are not deleted; only export of an encrypted backup file and recovery code is available after authentication when App Lock is enabled. Usage analytics, overseas transfer and AI feature consents are separate.

Google Play distributes the app, while Android provides platform functions such as installation, app-private storage, backup controls, and deletion. Separate processing by Google Play, Android, or a document provider you choose is governed by each provider's terms and policies. This policy describes only processing inside DoseWeek. The app does not send your health records to the developer or Google Play. Your records stay on the device unless you turn on an optional integration; sections 3 and 4 describe what each one does.

2. Information you store on your device

DoseWeek keeps the following information that you manually enter and confirm in app-private storage. Apart from the optional integrations, backups and exports described in this policy, it is processed only on your Android device to provide the app's features.

  • Weekly injection schedule, including product and region option, dose, weekday, time, and time-zone basis
  • Actual administration records, including date, time, dose, selected injection site, and optional note
  • Weekly schedule entries that you mark as skipped
  • Inventory count managed as remaining administrations
  • Weight and waist circumference that you enter manually
  • Side-effect logs, including symptom, severity, time, and optional note, and the change history of edited or deleted administration records, including the reason you enter
  • Settings you choose: accent color and appearance, reminder options including whether reminder text names your medication, and whether the app lock is on

3. Optional services and usage analytics

DoseWeek 1.0.5 does not use the following features or automatic processing in its default state. Every record is entered and confirmed manually by the user.

  • Collection or transmission of health records to a developer server, or automatic sharing with other companies or users
  • A DoseWeek account, developer-operated cloud storage, or automatic sync
  • Crash-reporting SDKs
  • Starting Firebase usage analytics without your optional consent
  • Generative AI
  • Health Connect write or delete access
  • Wearable apps or notifications pushed from a server

Health records, including schedules, administrations, notes and body measurements, stay on your device by default. The developer does not receive these records. Exports, backups and optional integrations are described separately. Optional usage analytics follows the rules below.

Acknowledging the required privacy notice does not permit analytics. Analytics is enabled only after separate consent to usage analytics and overseas transfer. Both optional choices are off by default, and analytics is disabled if either is refused or withdrawn.

Usage analytics is optional and initially off. Acknowledging the required privacy notice does not grant permission. Only after you opt in does Google Analytics for Firebase start and send automatic lifecycle and technical events, such as first opens, sessions, activity frequency, engagement time and app or operating-system updates, to Google. The developer uses usage reports to understand app use. DoseWeek adds no custom events.

Analytics can include Firebase-generated app-instance or installation identifiers, event times, app version, language, device brand, model and class, operating-system details including minor versions, and screen resolution. Google processes IP addresses for service operation and approximate location, including city; a city's derived coordinates are not your GPS location. No GPS or location permission is used for analytics. Google processes the data on its servers, potentially outside your country, and the developer can access reports. These records are not inherently anonymous.

DoseWeek does not send health records, medication names, doses, notes, typed or voice input, screen names or routes to analytics. It sets no user ID or custom user properties. Advertising IDs and Android ID (SSAID) collection, automatic screen reporting, Google signals and advertising personalization are disabled. Crashlytics and Performance Monitoring are not used.

Google Analytics user-level and event-level retention is set to 2 months, with reset on new activity off. Setting changes take 24 hours to apply; expired data is deleted in Google's monthly process, not exactly at the two-month instant. This setting does not govern standard aggregated reports and does not guarantee permanent availability of those reports.

Long-term storage of anonymous monthly totals is planned only after a privacy review and verified export that prevents identification. No such export has been made with real data yet; raw analytics is not treated as anonymous.

Consent to usage analytics is separate from consent to optional food-label recognition and its disclosed Google ML Kit metrics. Changing one choice does not change the other.

The Health Connect integration is read-only. DoseWeek reads the weight, height, body fat percentage, and lean body mass that you have shared, and requests no write or delete permission, so it cannot change records another app owns. What it reads is kept in app-private storage and is not sent to the developer.

With the Google Drive integration on, the app can upload an encrypted backup to your own Drive app-data folder. The developer holds no copy and no key. Section 4 describes what Drive can see and how the recovery code is handled.

With the calendar integration on, DoseWeek writes a 30-minute event for each upcoming injection day into the one calendar you choose, with only a title and a time. The title is “Personal reminder” by default and becomes “Injection day” only if you choose the descriptive title yourself. Medication, dose, injection site, and notes are never written. The app reads the list of calendars and only the events it marked as its own, and updates or removes only those. If the calendar belongs to an account such as Google or Samsung, that provider stores and syncs the events under its own privacy policy, including to people the calendar is shared with. The developer receives nothing. Turning the integration off can remove the upcoming events, and events left in the calendar after you uninstall the app stay there.

4. Storage, backup, and device transfer

All live records are stored locally in Android app-private storage and are excluded from Android system cloud backup and device-to-device transfer. You can manually save all app data to an encrypted backup file at a location you choose through Android's system file picker (SAF), and restore by selecting that file. This process needs no DoseWeek account and no broad storage permission, and it uses no network access. Backups can be restored only in DoseWeek for Android. DoseWeek on other platforms uses a different backup format, so backups cannot be moved between platforms.

If you select an external or cloud document provider, that provider stores the backup under its own privacy policy and terms. The DoseWeek developer receives neither the backup file nor its recovery code.

The backup uses AES-256-GCM authenticated encryption and a separate high-entropy recovery code. You make this file backup yourself; the only automatic option is the optional Google Drive backup described below, which is off by default. It is not a DoseWeek-operated cloud backup, and there is no code-reset service. Keep the file and code safe and separate. If the code is lost, the developer and support cannot reset it or decrypt the file. Until you confirm that you saved a file backup's recovery code, DoseWeek keeps that code on this device only, wrapped with a key held by the device, so that you can view it again; it is deleted once you confirm.

During restore, DoseWeek decrypts and completely validates the file, including its structure and data relationships, before atomically replacing current app data in one local transaction.

As an option, DoseWeek can also keep that encrypted backup in your own Google Drive app-data folder. Drive can see a file name containing a random identifier, the file size, the creation time, the payload version, the key version, and a random snapshot identifier. No medication name, dose, measurement, date of treatment, or note appears outside the encrypted envelope.

Encryption happens on the device before upload, with the same AES-256-GCM envelope the offline file export uses. The recovery code is never stored with the backup and is never sent anywhere. Until you confirm that you saved it, you can show the same code again; after that, DoseWeek does not show it again. You must re-enter it before any automatic backup can start. If it is lost, the developer cannot read or recover the backup.

Optional automatic backup is off by default; when it is on it runs about once a day when the device allows it, and does not promise an exact time. Deleting a backup from Drive is permanent, because Drive offers no trash for app-data files. Disconnecting stops future uploads: it does not delete backups already in Drive and does not delete records on the device, and destroying this device's copy of the recovery code is a separate, clearly labelled choice. Each backup that uploads changes, whether automatic or Back up now, is stored as a separate encrypted file. Earlier files stay until you delete them from Stored backups in the app or remove the app's data from your Google account, and they use your Google storage.

5. Ads in the free version

This section applies to versions of DoseWeek that are a free download with the optional Plus subscription. Free users see ads; Plus subscribers do not. Ads are shown by the Google Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob), and ad consent is handled by Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP). Both are provided by Google. No ad is shown until Google Play has confirmed whether you have Plus. Once Plus is confirmed, the app removes ads at once and does not start the ad SDK; ad requests already sent before that moment are not recalled.

DoseWeek requests only non-personalized ads. The app removes the advertising ID permission and the Android ad-services permissions for the advertising ID, attribution and topics, and it removes the install referrer service, so the SDK reads the advertising ID as zeros. Every ad request asks for non-personalized ads.

Non-personalized ads still involve processing. When the SDK requests, shows and measures an ad, Google receives your IP address, device and app information (such as device model, operating-system version, app version and language), ad interaction data (such as views and taps), diagnostic data and identifiers the SDK creates for this app. Google uses this information to serve ads, to limit how often an ad is shown, to prevent fraud and abuse and to measure ads. Google receives it as a recipient under its own terms and privacy policy, processes it on its servers, which can be outside your country, and keeps it for the periods Google sets; DoseWeek cannot shorten them. The data leaves the device over the internet while ads are requested, shown and measured. The developer sees only aggregated AdMob reports, such as ad views and earnings.

Where the law requires consent, for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland and some US states, Google's consent message appears before any ad is requested, and ads are requested only after the SDK reports that they may be. To find out whether a message is needed, the app asks Google's consent service at launch, for Free users only; this request also reaches Google and is separate from ad requests. You can review or change your choice at any time in Settings > Privacy choices for ads; where your region needs no choice, that screen says so. The one-time notice that DoseWeek is now free with ads only informs you; reading it is not consent. If ad processing is not allowed, because you declined, the form could not load or the consent service is unavailable, DoseWeek shows no ads and every Free feature keeps working. DoseWeek for Android is still only for people aged 18 or older; the age confirmation is unchanged and separate from ad consent, usage analytics and overseas transfer.

DoseWeek never passes health records, notes, medication names, doses, dates, meals or screen names to the ad SDK, and ad requests carry no keywords, content links or custom targeting. The app's ad code does not read your records. Like any SDK, Google's SDKs run inside the app process with the same technical access to the app's files as the app itself. DoseWeek does not give them your records, but it cannot technically prevent that access, so this boundary depends on Google's SDKs behaving as Google documents them.

AdMob is not linked to Firebase or Google Analytics. Ad consent and analytics consent (section 3) are separate: neither choice grants or withdraws the other, and the analytics settings keep advertising storage, ad user data and ad personalization denied. To report an ad, tap the AdChoices icon on the ad, or use Report an ad in Settings, which opens an email to support with the time and the screen where the ad appeared. It adds no health data; please do not add any.

6. Plus subscription and purchase verification

Plus is an optional auto-renewing subscription, monthly or annual, sold through Google Play Billing. The price and billing period are shown in the app and on Google Play before you buy. Google Play processes the payment and your Google account under its own terms; DoseWeek does not receive your payment details.

To unlock Plus, the app checks the signed purchase data from Google Play and has the developer's purchase-verification server confirm it. The app sends the server only the app's package name, the product and the purchase token that Google Play issued, never health records or other app content. The server asks Google Play for the purchase's current state, acknowledges a new purchase and returns the state to the app. Google Play also notifies the server when a subscription renews, is cancelled, expires or is refunded. As with any internet connection, the server and its hosting provider see the device's IP address; the server does not keep it in its purchase records.

The server stores only a one-way hash of the purchase token, with the product, subscription state, relevant times (such as expiry and grace period) and notification identifiers. It stores no health data, no device identifier and no name or email address, and it never writes the raw token to its logs. The server location and operator details are listed here before this version is released. How long the server keeps these records is also listed here before this version is released.

If Google Play or the server cannot be reached, the app keeps using its last verified Plus state for a limited time, at most seven days after the last verification; otherwise it shows no ads and keeps Plus tools locked until the check succeeds. A pending purchase does not unlock Plus until Google Play completes it.

The app keeps a short cache of the last verified subscription state, ad-frequency counters, the time of the last Plus offer and the 30-minute ad-free pass in its preferences on the device. These are not health data, are not included in the encrypted backup and are not sent anywhere by DoseWeek. Plus tool data (meal templates, visit summary settings and the Today layout) is kept in a protected app-private file that is excluded from Android backup and device transfer and, in this version, is not part of the encrypted backup. Delete all data in Settings removes Plus tool data but keeps the ad counters and subscription cache, so deleting records does not reset ad limits; it does not cancel a subscription.

Cancel or change your plan in Google Play under Payments & subscriptions > Subscriptions, which you can open from Settings in DoseWeek. Restore purchases in Settings checks Google Play for subscriptions bought with the same Google account; the app also checks when it starts and when you return to it. Uninstalling the app does not cancel a subscription. Buying DoseWeek when it was a paid app does not create a Plus subscription, and the app does not read that purchase or the install date to decide about Plus or ads.

7. Retention and deletion

Records remain in app-private storage on that device until you delete them. Use Delete all data in Settings, or uninstall the app, to have Android delete DoseWeek's local app data. There is no developer-server copy.

Backup files and CSV export files saved outside the app are not deleted automatically when app data is deleted or the app is uninstalled. Delete them separately through Android's file app or their document provider when needed.

If you used Google Drive backup, delete those copies from the backup list inside the app, or by removing the app's data from your Google account. Uninstalling DoseWeek does not remove them.

You can delete local app data and Drive backups separately. Backups and exports saved with another provider follow that provider's controls. Uninstalling DoseWeek or disconnecting Drive does not itself request deletion of Drive backups or Google food-label SDK diagnostic and usage metrics. Google's processing and retention of those metrics follow Google's terms; DoseWeek cannot directly delete them or state Google's retention period.

If you refuse either optional consent, analytics stays off and core functions remain available. You can withdraw either consent in Settings. Withdrawal stops future analytics collection but does not automatically delete records already sent to Google.

Withdrawing analytics consent stops future collection and requests a reset of local analytics data and its identifier when the SDK is already initialized. If data-deletion recovery occurs before the SDK is initialized, a local reset is requested first when you later consent again to analytics and overseas transfer and activate the SDK. This is not confirmation of Google server deletion or completed disk cleanup.

While analytics is on, the app can show you an analytics deletion request code: open Settings > Usage analytics (optional), open “Analytics deletion request code” and tap “View deletion request code”. If Settings shows a list of sections, as on some wide screens, select “Data management” first. This code contains the app-instance identifier used to locate analytics records for the current installation. If you wish, copy it before turning analytics off or deleting app data and request deletion at wonyoung@wonyoungchoi.dev. Viewing or copying the code does not send a deletion request. Do not send health records.

The code can be shown only while analytics is on. After you turn analytics off or delete app data, that code can no longer be shown or recovered. Without the previous identifier, we may be unable to locate records already sent for that installation. You can still withdraw analytics consent at any time.

8. Security

DoseWeek uses Android app-private storage, excludes app data from system backup and device transfer, and does not transmit health records through its own network connection unless you turn on Google Drive backup, which uploads only the encrypted envelope described in section 4. User-created backups use AES-256-GCM authenticated encryption, but their security also depends on safeguarding the backup file and separate recovery code. Keep your device lock and Android security updates enabled, and delete app data before giving the device to someone else.

The optional app lock relies on your device's own screen lock or biometrics through Android's system prompt; DoseWeek stores no biometric data and hides its content in the app switcher while the lock is on. Optional reminders are scheduled only on the device: by default their text reveals nothing about your treatment, the medication name appears only if you enable it, dose amounts never appear, and tapping a reminder only opens the app.

The home-screen widget shows a schedule date, status and day count. Other people who can see your unlocked home screen may see this information. Enabling App Lock hides the schedule in the widget. A medication name appears only when you enable both reminder details and medication-name display; dose amounts never appear. The widget is read-only: tapping it only opens the app and never records an administration. Widget processing stays on the device and makes no separate network transmissions.

When cloud backup is on, this device's copy of the recovery code is sealed with a non-exportable key held in the Android hardware keystore, and transport is TLS to Google's API hosts only.

10. Features added in DoseWeek 1.0.0 (versionCode 12)

This section describes how the features added in DoseWeek 1.0.0 (versionCode 12) for Android handle your data. The optional calendar sync and backup wording in sections 3 and 4 describes the same version. Effective date: September 23, 2026.

Meal records: the meal name, date, optional time and meal kind you enter, and the energy, protein, carbohydrate and fat totals you know, are kept in app-private storage. A value you leave blank stays unknown and is not calculated. Meal records are included in the encrypted backup, can be written to a separate plaintext CSV file only when you ask for it, and are not part of the visit summary. The developer receives no meal information.

Schedules and body charts: a plan can store a list of injection dates you choose instead of a repeating interval, and the weekly weight-change and BMI charts can show either a published general reference or a personal target you set. The chosen reference and target value are stored on the device and are included in the encrypted backup. A reference is general population information, not advice about your treatment.

Backup format: the new features raise the format number of the encrypted backup file. Backups written by earlier versions can still be restored, but a backup written by the new version cannot be restored by an earlier version. Calendar event links and unsaved drafts are not included in a backup. Your choice of reference medication for the estimate is not included in a backup; choose it again after a restore.

Food data tables: this version includes an offline, read-only food table built from public data (USDA FoodData Central, public domain under CC0 1.0 with attribution requested; UK CoFID under the Open Government Licence v3.0; the Japanese MEXT Standard Tables of Food Composition under the MEXT data-use statement and site terms; Korean MFDS data under the data.go.kr grant with no use restriction). Food search runs only on the device: what you type and the results are not sent anywhere, and the table is not downloaded or updated over the network. Your market and search-language choices and the foods you save as favorites from the table are stored on the device and included in the encrypted backup. Values are shown as the source reports them and are general reference information. The app lists each source, licence and attribution. Declining consent preserves manual entry. No recognition starts merely by installing or opening DoseWeek.
