DoseWeek · iOS 1.0.5

Help, without the guesswork.

This text takes effect on October 3, 2026. The parts about accounts, sync, Plus, ads and other new features describe the first DoseWeek version that has them (the next iOS update) and apply from the release of that version. Earlier versions do not have these features. You may optionally sign in to a DoseWeek account with Apple or Google. Existing local-record features remain available without sign-in. Linking a verified Plus subscription from either store to the same DoseWeek account enables encrypted sync on both platforms. Verified Plus also shares ad removal and Plus tools across both platforms. To revoke Apple sign-in, the server may retain an Apple refresh token encrypted with a separate server key, distinct from health-record end-to-end keys.

Getting started

How to use DoseWeek in six steps, from setup to your first record and backup.

  1. Open DoseWeek and set it up

    Read the privacy notice and choose whether to allow optional usage analytics; every core feature works without it. Then tap Get Started, or choose Restore from backup, Import records from another app or Manage records without a plan.

    Where are my records, and can support see them? →
  2. Set your schedule

    Choose your medication and dose, then pick Repeat every few days or Pick dates on a calendar; you can change the plan later in Settings > Treatment plan. Reminders and Apple Health reading are optional.

    Choose your own injection dates →
  3. Record an injection

    On Today, tap Review injection, answer Yes, I did, then check the details and save. Your records appear in History, where you can also add a past injection or correct a record.

    Correct a record →
  4. Record meals

    Open Body and switch to Meals. Enter only the values you know, or use Search food to find items in the built-in offline food list.

    Record meals →
  5. Import earlier records

    Choose Import records from another app in History or in Settings > Data management. Review the records from your CSV, TSV or JSON file; only the ones you select are added.

  6. Backups, reminders and privacy choices

    In Settings, export an encrypted backup, adjust Notifications and change Usage analytics (optional) at any time. Quick answers

FAQ and troubleshooting

The most common questions about records, Apple Health, reminders, backups, sensitive exports, App Lock, on-device AI, Plus and ads.

Where are my records, and can support see them?

Health records, including schedules, administrations, notes and body measurements, stay on your device by default. The developer does not receive them in readable form; if you turn on account sync, they are sent to the server only end-to-end encrypted, so the developer cannot read them. Content you consent to and send to the AI record assistant (Pro) is the exception to end-to-end encryption: the DoseWeek server and Google can read it while an answer is being made. Google can also read it for as long as Google holds it: in memory for up to 24 hours, or for up to 90 days when Google’s abuse checks flag a request, which authorized Google staff may then review. Exports, backups and optional integrations are described separately. Optional usage analytics follows the rules below.

Usage analytics is optional and initially off. Acknowledging the required privacy notice does not grant permission. Only after you opt in does Google Analytics for Firebase start and send automatic lifecycle and technical events, such as first opens, sessions, activity frequency, engagement time and app or operating-system updates, to Google. The developer uses usage reports to understand app use. DoseWeek adds no custom events.

Before deleting the app or moving to another device, create and export an encrypted backup if you want to keep your records. A backup made on iPhone or iPad can be restored only in DoseWeek on iPhone or iPad. DoseWeek on other platforms uses different backup formats, so backup files cannot be moved between platforms.

What does DoseWeek read from Apple Health?

Apple Health access is read-only and limited to exactly five body metrics you permit: weight, BMI, body fat percentage, lean body mass, and waist circumference. DoseWeek never writes to Apple Health.

You can change access in iOS Settings at any time. Manual body-metric entry remains available.

Why did a reminder not arrive?

DoseWeek schedules local notifications directly on your device and does not use a push server. Check that notifications are allowed for DoseWeek in iOS Settings, then review the reminder options in the app.

Notifications never include dose amounts. You can choose whether a medication name appears.

How do backups and recovery codes work?

The file-backup details below describe manual export and import. Plus automatic server backup is a separate path.

A backup is created only when you choose to make one. It is encrypted on device with AES-256-GCM and leaves the app only when you explicitly export it through the system file picker, saving it to a location you choose in the Files app. The live database and local backup envelope are excluded from device backups.

Keep the exported file and its recovery code secure. The developer has access to neither and cannot recover a lost code or decrypt the file for you. Never share either with support.

While Plus is active, you can separately consent to turn on automatic server backup and sync. App records are end-to-end encrypted on the device; the server cannot read the backed-up contents. Free users can still manually export and import files and use the in-app backup reminder.

DoseWeek does not use iCloud or CloudKit to back up app data. A backup file you manually export to a cloud location you choose is a separate path governed by that provider.

If you turn it on separately, DoseWeek encrypts all your records on the device (injection plans and their changes, dose records, meals, body measurements, supplies and import history) together with supported settings, for automatic backup and sync across both platforms. The settings that sync are appearance (colour and light or dark mode), the weight-change unit, the body map view, whether injection reminders are on, whether reminders show details or the medication name, recording without a plan, supply tracking, and your food search market and search-language choices. When one of these is changed on another device, this device applies it and tells you once about an important change, such as reminders or supply tracking. Sync never turns on an AI feature: you turn it on yourself on each device. Permissions, connections and display choices that belong to a single device are not synced: notification permission, the app lock, health-data, calendar and cloud-storage connections, and widget appearance. When you restore from Settings, the saved settings are applied and the app tells you which settings changed. This is end-to-end encryption: the server keeps only encrypted snapshots and the account and purchase records it needs. Your data key is stored only in a form locked by your recovery code, and the recovery code stays on your devices, so the operator cannot read your health records. The server handles account and provider identifiers, verified Plus state, ciphertext, versions and minimal access metadata, but receives neither readable health records nor your recovery code. A new device needs the recovery code; if it is lost, the developer cannot recover the encrypted data. An empty device may restore after code confirmation; a device with existing records requires a preview and merge confirmation. To recheck subscription status with the store, the server also keeps keyed purchase digests and encrypted lookup references containing an Apple original transaction ID or store purchase token. References use AES-256-GCM with a server-only key separate from the end-to-end health-record keys and are used only for store checks. Account deletion erases references; expired references are pruned after 30 days. Keyed digest markers that prevent duplicate purchase linking are kept for 5 years as records of payment and supply under Article 6 of the Enforcement Decree of the Korean Act on the Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce, and are then deleted. Content you consent to and send to the AI record assistant (Pro) is the exception to end-to-end encryption: the DoseWeek server and Google can read it while an answer is being made. Google can also read it for as long as Google holds it: in memory for up to 24 hours, or for up to 90 days when Google’s abuse checks flag a request, which authorized Google staff may then review.

Are PDF and CSV exports encrypted?

PDF and CSV exports are plaintext files generated on device only when you request them. They are delivered to the destination you choose in the system share sheet. After sharing, DoseWeek and the developer cannot control copies or retention.

Choose a trusted destination, then store and delete exported files securely. An encrypted DoseWeek backup is different from a plaintext PDF or CSV.

Why is an AI feature unavailable?

Optional AI entry and Visit Prep use only Apple Foundation Models’ on-device SystemLanguageModel.default. Availability depends on the device, Apple Intelligence status, and supported system language. These two on-device features use no Private Cloud Compute, server model, or third-party model. The AI record assistant (Pro) is a different feature: it is processed on a server, with a third-party model. Only after your separate consent, what you confirm and send goes through the DoseWeek server to Google Cloud Vertex AI (Global, no fixed country), where the Gemini model by Google creates the answer. It needs an internet connection and can be paused for a while; the app tells you when that happens. The Privacy Policy describes it under “AI record assistant (Pro)”.

This part applies from the first DoseWeek version that has the AI record assistant (the next iOS update). Until that version is released, the app has no AI record assistant and sends nothing to Google for it.

If the model is unavailable, DoseWeek explains the fallback and the normal manual entry or deterministic Visit Prep view remains available.

What if Face ID or the device passcode becomes unavailable?

Set a passcode again in iOS Settings, return to DoseWeek, then unlock. App Lock cannot be turned off without authentication. Support cannot unlock the app remotely.

How do I delete my records?

Use Settings > Delete All Local Records in DoseWeek. DoseWeek immediately removes the records and on-device recovery code, then securely cleans the private database files.

If iOS temporarily prevents that cleanup, the app tells you to run the deletion again or relaunch DoseWeek and automatically retries before reopening the database. Deleted records are not restored. Manage exported files and calendar-provider copies separately.

You may decline analytics in the notice and turn it off at any time in Settings > Usage analytics (optional). Core app functions remain available. Turning it off stops future analytics collection and requests a reset of local analytics data and its app-instance identifier. It does not delete data already sent to Google. If analytics has not started in the current app session, this reset is deferred until you turn analytics on again. Deleting local app data also is not a server-deletion request. Contact the address in this policy for data-handling or deletion inquiries.

While analytics is on, the app can show you an analytics deletion request code: open Settings > Usage analytics (optional) and tap “View deletion request code”. This code contains the app-instance identifier used to locate analytics records for the current installation. If you wish, copy it before turning analytics off or deleting app data and request deletion at wonyoung@wonyoungchoi.dev. Viewing or copying the code does not send a deletion request. Do not send health records.

The code can be shown only while analytics is on. After you turn analytics off or delete app data, that code can no longer be shown or recovered. Without the previous identifier, we may be unable to locate records already sent for that installation. You can still withdraw analytics consent at any time.

The encrypted server copy is kept while Plus is active. It is deleted 30 days after the end of the last Plus period that the server verified with the App Store or another supported store. Before deleting, the server checks with the store again and keeps the copy if Plus was renewed. If the store cannot be reached, deletion may be postponed by up to 7 days. At any time you can delete only the server copy (reset sync), which keeps your account and the records on your devices, or delete the whole account. If you request direct deletion or withdraw consent, the 30-day waiting period and store-outage extension do not apply. Access stops immediately and the data covered by the request is erased from live storage without delay. Deleting only the server copy keeps the account. Completion is reported after the actual result is confirmed. Server backups are kept for at most 7 days, so deleted data leaves those backups within 7 days. Records on your devices and files you exported or stored separately, for example in a cloud storage service, must be deleted separately. Deleting the DoseWeek account does not cancel a store subscription. DoseWeek account and server-data deletion is reported separately from provider disconnection. If disconnection cannot be confirmed, you receive instructions for manual removal; no automatic retry is promised. After account deletion, the server keeps only a keyed marker of each purchase that was linked to the account, without any link to the account, so that the purchase cannot silently move to another account. This marker is kept for 5 years as a record of payment and supply under Article 6 of the Enforcement Decree of the Korean Act on the Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce, and is then deleted. Records of consumer complaints and dispute handling, such as support requests, are kept for 3 years under the same article. If you bought Plus on another supported store, the separate purchase-verification server keeps its purchase records (a one-way hash of the purchase token, the product, subscription state and times) until 90 days after the latest of the paid-period end, grace-period end and last check, and notification identifiers for 30 days; these records are not linked to a DoseWeek account and are deleted on that schedule. These purchase-verification records hold only the verification state, with no price, payment method or order details, so they are not payment records. Payment records are kept by the store that sold the subscription.

Email wonyoung@wonyoungchoi.dev to request deletion of your DoseWeek account and associated server data without reinstalling the app or subscribing to Plus. Identify the sign-in provider and, if known, your DoseWeek account ID. We explain identity verification by email. Do not send passwords, recovery codes, health records or purchase tokens. Composing or sending the email does not confirm deletion. Account/server-data erasure and provider disconnection outcomes are confirmed and communicated separately. If disconnection cannot be confirmed, we explain manual removal in the provider’s account settings. Canceling a subscription is not a condition of this request; manage store billing separately.

Choose dates and times

Available in DoseWeek iOS 1.0.5 and later.

Tap a date or time to open the system picker. Use the month or year control for older dates, and AM/PM when shown. Cancel keeps your entry; Done changes the draft. Save the record to keep your changes.

Correct a record

Available in DoseWeek iOS 1.0.5 and later.

In History, choose the date, then Edit on the record. Save to update that same record. If you change its date, the confirmation helps you find it on the new date. Imported Apple Health measurements are read-only: correct them in the original app.

Add a past injection

Available in DoseWeek iOS 1.0.5 and later.

In History, choose a past date and add a past administration. Confirm the medicine, dose, unit, and actual time before saving. You can manage records without creating a plan. Records without a plan do not change schedules or stock. For records linked to a plan, check whether stock will be deducted. Restoring an encrypted backup replaces data; it is a separate action.

Find missing measurements

Available in DoseWeek iOS 1.0.5 and later.

First check the original app, then Apple Health. In DoseWeek, open Body, check the metric and period, and refresh. A blank result does not tell you whether read access was allowed. The measurement date is when it was measured, not when DoseWeek imported it.

Understand left and right

Available in DoseWeek iOS 1.0.5 and later.

Left and right always mean your own body. My body and Facing me change only how the diagram is displayed. Your selection and saved records keep the same body side. Check the selected name or open the site list to choose by text. You can change the view in Settings too.

Record meals

Available in DoseWeek iOS 1.0.5 and later.

Open Body and switch to Meals. Enter the food name and the totals you know — energy, protein, carbohydrate, fat — and leave anything you do not know blank; a blank value stays unknown and is never estimated. A meal holds up to 100 items and you can keep up to 1,000 favorites. You can also search the built-in offline food table, made from public food data of the United States, the United Kingdom, Japan and Korea. You can now search for 200 foods in the list using names in 17 languages. Search starts with all supported languages; choose specific languages in Settings if you prefer. Searching works without a network connection, values appear as the source reports them, and the app lists each source and licence; foods the table does not cover are typed in as before. Meal records stay on the device, travel in the encrypted backup, have their own CSV export, and are excluded from Visit Prep and the PDF export. They are not nutrition advice or a prescription.

Choose your own injection dates

Available in DoseWeek iOS 1.0.5 and later.

In the plan editor, switch the schedule mode to chosen dates and select the days in the calendar, up to one year ahead. The preview lists what will be scheduled. A future date closer than the minimum interval in the verified medicine information is blocked at save; records you already made in the past are never blocked. After the last chosen date the plan stays active and offers to add more dates.

Reference ranges on body charts

Available in DoseWeek iOS 1.0.5 and later.

The weekly weight-change and BMI charts can show a published general reference (WHO, CDC, NHS, NICE, the Korean Society for the Study of Obesity, the Japan Society for the Study of Obesity) or a personal target you set. Nothing is preselected and the label carries only the range and its source, never a category word. In percent mode an official band is converted for each interval, with a note saying so. A reference is general population information, not advice about your treatment.

Calendar sync

Available in DoseWeek iOS 1.0.5 and later.

Calendar sync is off until you turn it on in Settings, and iOS asks for full calendar access at that point. Once on, DoseWeek writes up to 24 upcoming injection days within the next 84 days as 30-minute events in the one calendar you choose. The title is “Personal reminder” unless you choose “Injection day”; medication, dose, site, notes and alerts are never written. Only the events DoseWeek created are updated or removed, and an event you edit yourself is left alone from then on. If the calendar belongs to an account such as iCloud, Google or Exchange, that account stores and syncs the events under its own policy, including to anyone the calendar is shared with.

Supported devices

This applies to DoseWeek iOS 1.0.5 and later.

Use DoseWeek on iPhone and iPad with iOS 26.0 or later. On iPhone, use the app in portrait; you can turn a full-screen chart sideways.

Bring records from another app

Available in DoseWeek iOS 1.0.5 and later.

You can import past administrations from a CSV, TSV or JSON file, or paste a table. One import takes up to 10,000 records, a source file larger than 10 MB is refused rather than cut, and a saved review draft is limited to 4 MiB on iPhone and iPad. Symptom and unclassified rows cannot be saved yet and must be deselected; their source text is kept so nothing is lost. Rows that may be duplicates are marked for review and nothing is saved until you confirm. This is not a backup restore. The step-by-step import guide, from the AI prompt to adding the reviewed records, is at doseweek-legal.wonyoungchoi.dev/import/.

Is DoseWeek free?

Yes. DoseWeek is a free download, and Free users see ads. Recording, History, Body charts, the estimate, fixed 4, 8 and 12-week Visit Prep, meals, manual file exports and imports, backup reminders, calendar sync, reminders, widgets and App Lock remain free.

Plus is an optional auto-renewing subscription, monthly or annual, that removes ads and adds extra tools. The price and billing period are shown in the app and on the App Store before you buy.

What does Plus add, and what stays free?

Plus removes ads and adds four tools: period summaries that compare a week or month with the one before; a visit report editor for the period (up to 26 weeks or a custom range of up to 365 days), sections, order and layout; Today layout presets for the order and visibility of Today's extra cards; and meal templates that open as an unsaved draft for you to review. The tools use only values you recorded and give no medical advice.

Why do I see ads, and how can I use DoseWeek without ads for up to 48 hours?

Ads keep the free version free. Free users see a banner on History and in the Measurements section of Body, and occasionally a full-screen ad after closing certain help or food-settings sheets. Ads never appear on Today, in the record flow or on safety notices. Ads are non-personalized, and Plus removes them.

You can choose to watch one ad and then use DoseWeek without ads for 24 hours. The offer sits next to the Ad label on History and Body and in Settings, and the condition is shown before the ad starts. It is optional and not a purchase: closing the ad early grants nothing, and declining changes no Free feature. You can earn at most two rewards in any rolling 24 hours, and remaining ad-free time never exceeds 48 hours. While a pass is active, another rewarded ad is offered only when 24 hours or less remain. The pass pauses if the device clock is set back more than five minutes from the latest time the app has seen. While the pass is active, automatic ads and Plus subscription offers are hidden.

I bought DoseWeek before it was free. What changes for me?

How do I change my ad privacy choices or report an ad?

Open Settings > Privacy choices for ads. Where the law requires a choice, for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland or some US states, this opens Google's consent form so you can change your choice; elsewhere it explains that no choice is needed in your region. Ad choices are separate from Usage analytics (optional): changing one does not change the other. If ad processing is not allowed, DoseWeek shows no ads and every Free feature keeps working.

To report an inappropriate ad, tap the AdChoices icon on the ad, or use Report an ad in Settings, which opens an email with the time and screen. Do not add health details. Section 11 of the privacy policy explains what Google processes for ads.
